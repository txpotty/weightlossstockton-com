    How Wellness & Longevity Care Works in Modesto

    Wellness and longevity care here means seven specific treatments, each tied to a goal such as chronic pain, a suspected parasitic infection, or interest in research-informed peptides. The first step is naming the goal and the symptoms behind it. Evidence comes next. A few are approved drugs used in new ways, while others are compounded without FDA approval. Primary care stays in the picture.

    Low Dose Naltrexone

    Naltrexone is FDA-approved at 50 mg for alcohol and opioid use disorders, while low dose naltrexone, usually 1.5 to 4.5 mg at bedtime, is prescribed off-label. Researchers believe a short receptor block and lower inflammation may explain its effects. Studies are small. Small studies in fibromyalgia and certain autoimmune conditions show promise but are not definitive. It cannot be taken with opioids.

    Naltrexone Gum in Modesto

    Naltrexone gum is a compounded form that delivers a small dose through the lining of the mouth, and some providers use it as a flexible, as-needed option to reduce cravings in specific situations. This form lacks FDA approval. Research is limited. Your provider explains how and when to chew it, and how long to keep it in your mouth. Opioid use and liver problems are screened first.

    Antiparasitic Protocol, Ivermectin and Mebendazole

    Ivermectin is FDA-approved to treat certain parasitic worm infections such as strongyloidiasis and onchocerciasis, and it is also used for scabies and head lice. Mebendazole treats intestinal worms such as pinworm, roundworm, whipworm and hookworm. Travel history matters. Your provider picks the drug and dose based on the likely parasite and your exposure. These are prescribed only for their anti-parasitic uses.

    Anti-Aging and Immune Support Peptides in Modesto

    Peptides are short chains of amino acids that act as signaling molecules, and our Anti-Aging Peptides and Immune Support Peptides are compounded, research-informed therapies that are not FDA-approved for these uses. Evidence ranges from early human studies to mostly laboratory research. Most are injected. Expect a tiny needle under the skin a few times a week. FDA rules on compounded peptides can change availability.

    How a Provider Chooses Between Options

    Chronic pain or inflammation may point toward low dose naltrexone, while a suspected parasitic infection based on symptoms, travel or exposure points toward ivermectin, mebendazole or the Antiparasitic Protocol. Peptides come up when your goal is longevity or immune support and you accept the uncertainty. Safety comes first. Your provider weighs interactions, evidence and your preferences, and may recommend labs first. Goal decides the starting point.

    Side Effects and Safety in Modesto

    Low dose naltrexone may cause vivid dreams, insomnia or headache in the first weeks, which often ease or improve with morning dosing. Antiparasitics can bring nausea, dizziness, belly pain or loose stools. Peptides vary. Expect possible redness at the injection site, plus peptide-specific effects your provider explains. Report anything unusual.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your provider asks about your goal, symptoms, travel, opioid use, alcohol, liver and kidney health, pregnancy and every medication you take before recommending anything. Any opioid use, including tramadol or codeine, rules out naltrexone because it can trigger sudden withdrawal. Pregnancy changes options. Antiparasitics are usually avoided during pregnancy. Liver disease needs a closer look.
