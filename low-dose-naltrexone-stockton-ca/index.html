    How Low Dose Naltrexone Works for Patients in Stockton, CA

    How Low Dose Naltrexone Works in Stockton

    Standard naltrexone is a 50 mg tablet, FDA-approved for alcohol and opioid use disorder, and it blocks opioid receptors for most of the day. Low dose naltrexone drops that to somewhere between 1.5 and 4.5 mg, taken at night. The block lasts a few hours. Researchers think the short blockade may lead the body to turn up its own endorphin signaling once the drug wears off, though that idea is still being tested. LDN also seems to quiet microglia, immune cells in the brain and spinal cord linked to pain and inflammation. That is the theory, not settled science.

    Who Low Dose Naltrexone Is For

    Most people who ask about LDN live with fibromyalgia or a long-running inflammatory condition that has not responded well to the usual treatments. Small studies in fibromyalgia and Crohn's disease are the most cited evidence. Some patients also ask about it alongside a weight management plan, though it is not a weight loss drug. Anyone taking opioids is out. The provider wants to know what you hope LDN will change and compares that against what the published studies looked at, which is usually pain and fatigue.

    LDN Dosing and Schedule in Stockton

    A common start is 1.5 mg at bedtime, in a compounded capsule or a liquid you measure with an oral syringe. The dose then climbs slowly, often 1.5 mg at a time every week or two, toward a maintenance range of 3 to 4.5 mg. Night dosing lines the short receptor block up with sleep. Vivid dreams can follow. If they keep you up, your provider may move the dose to the morning, which a fair number of patients end up preferring after the first month.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    Weeks one and two are mostly about sleep. Expect strange, very vivid dreams, along with a few nights of lighter sleep, especially right after the dose goes up. By weeks three and four most patients are near their target dose. Patients who respond usually describe a gradual change between week 4 and week 12, such as fewer bad pain days, rather than one morning when everything is different. Your provider checks in during titration. Around month three, you and the provider decide whether it has earned a place in your routine.

    Side Effects of LDN in Stockton

    Vivid dreams and insomnia top the list of side effects in published studies, with headache and mild nausea showing up a little less often. These usually ease within a week or two of starting or increasing. The bigger issue is opioids. Because it blocks opioid receptors, LDN can make pain pills like hydrocodone or tramadol useless and can cause withdrawal in regular users. Report dark urine or yellowing skin through secure messaging the same day.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your intake form asks for every prescription and supplement you take, with a specific question about tramadol and codeine cough syrup because people forget those count as opioids. Liver history matters too. If you have active liver disease or are pregnant, LDN is usually off the table. Nothing is prescribed unless the provider thinks the likely benefit beats the risk in your case. Some patients are asked to upload a recent liver panel showing ALT and AST values.

    How LDN Compares to Related Options in Stockton

    Full-dose naltrexone at 50 mg is FDA-approved for alcohol and opioid use disorder and blocks receptors around the clock, which is a different effect from the short nightly block of LDN. Some patients ask about naltrexone gum, a newer compounded format with very little research behind it. On weight loss, GLP-1 drugs win easily. Semaglutide averaged about 15 percent weight loss in the STEP trials, while LDN has no large weight loss trials at all. LDN can sometimes run alongside other treatments once your provider has checked for interactions.
