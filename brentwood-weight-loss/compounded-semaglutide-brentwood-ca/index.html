    What Compounded Semaglutide Is in Brentwood

    Compounded semaglutide is prepared by a licensed compounding pharmacy for an individual patient rather than mass produced. It uses the same active ingredient as Wegovy and Ozempic. It is not FDA-approved. That means the FDA has not reviewed compounded semaglutide for safety or effectiveness, and it does not inspect each batch the way it oversees brand-name manufacturing. Federal rules limit compounding copies of approved drugs, so a provider must document a clinical reason.

    Who Compounded Semaglutide Is For

    Candidates are adults with a BMI of 30 or higher, or 27 or higher with a weight-related condition. It can make sense for a patient who needs a smaller step between standard doses. Some patients prefer brand-name. Choosing FDA-approved Wegovy for its approval status is a perfectly reasonable decision. The provider weighs your coverage, your past side effects and any documented need for a personalized dose before recommending one path or the other.

    Dosing and Schedule in Brentwood

    Injections happen once a week under the skin of the belly or thigh, using a small syringe and a multi-dose vial. Treatment usually starts around 0.25 mg weekly. Units are not milligrams. Your dosing chart translates each milligram dose into syringe units for your specific vial concentration, because a 2.5 mg per mL vial and a 5 mg per mL vial need very different unit counts for the same dose. Your provider may use smaller steps if side effects are bothersome.

    What to Expect Week by Week

    The first month at a low dose usually brings only modest appetite changes. Mild queasiness is common in the day or two after an injection. Portions shrink by month two. The scale tends to move more once the dose passes 1 mg. Clinical trial results such as STEP 1 were based on brand-name semaglutide, so outcomes with compounded versions have not been studied the same way and your provider tracks your own progress closely.

    Side Effects of Compounded Semaglutide in Brentwood

    Nausea, diarrhea, constipation and vomiting are the most common side effects, mostly after starting or raising a dose. Dosing mistakes can cause severe versions of these symptoms. Check every syringe twice. Rarer but serious risks include pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, kidney injury from dehydration and low blood sugar when combined with insulin or a sulfonylurea. Semaglutide carries a boxed warning about thyroid C-cell tumors seen in rodents.

    Eligibility and Screening

    The intake asks about a personal or family history of medullary thyroid cancer or MEN2, which rules semaglutide out. Your provider also checks for past pancreatitis, gallbladder disease, diabetic retinopathy, severe stomach emptying problems and any history of an eating disorder before writing a prescription. Pregnancy is a hard stop. Expect a question about your comfort drawing medication from a vial.

    Compounded Semaglutide Compared With Wegovy in Brentwood

    Wegovy is FDA-approved, comes in prefilled pens with fixed doses and has years of trial data. Compounded semaglutide is not FDA-approved and usually comes in vials. Insurance treats them differently. Some plans cover Wegovy with a prior authorization, while compounded semaglutide is almost never covered, which often shapes the decision more than anything else. You will get a straight comparison before choosing.
