    How Mebendazole Treatment Works in Livermore, CA

    How Mebendazole Works in Livermore

    Mebendazole blocks a worm's ability to take in glucose by disrupting tiny structures inside its cells called microtubules. Without energy, the worms die. They pass in the stool over the following days, and most people never notice them, which is normal and does not mean the drug failed. Only a small amount is absorbed into the blood. In the US, it is sold as an FDA-approved chewable tablet.

    Who Mebendazole Is For

    Adults usually ask for it because of pinworm. The giveaway is anal itching at night, often in a household where school-age children brought it home from a classroom or daycare. It also treats roundworm, whipworm and hookworm, which are more often linked to travel or certain environments. Adults with a positive test qualify. Weight loss and cleanses are not on the list.

    Mebendazole Dosing and Schedule in Livermore

    For pinworm, the usual plan is a single 100 mg chewable tablet, repeated in two weeks. That second dose catches worms that hatched from eggs the first dose could not reach. Other intestinal worms are often treated with 100 mg twice daily for three days, followed by a stool test in some cases. Chew it fully. You can take it with or without food.

    What to Expect After Taking Mebendazole

    Most people feel nothing after the dose. Some get mild stomach discomfort. Itching usually eases within a few days, and it can take another week for the skin to calm down completely, especially if it was scratched a lot. You may not see worms. Put the two week dose in your phone calendar the day you take the first.

    Side Effects of Mebendazole in Livermore

    Because so little is absorbed, mebendazole is well tolerated at short courses. Possible side effects include abdominal pain, diarrhea, gas and nausea. Rash is rare. Extended high-dose use for other purposes can affect blood counts and the liver, so this program uses only standard short courses. Report fever or a spreading rash the same day.

    Eligibility and Screening

    Your intake asks about symptoms, timing, household members, recent travel and any test results. The provider will ask about pregnancy. It is usually not given early in pregnancy, and the provider checks breastfeeding status, liver history and current medications before deciding. Metronidazole is the big interaction, since combining the two has been linked to rare severe skin reactions. Non-pinworm infections usually need a stool test first.

    How Mebendazole Compares to Other Options in Livermore

    Albendazole works the same way and is sometimes used for the same infections. Pyrantel pamoate, sold without a prescription, also treats pinworm by paralyzing the worms. For pinworm, ivermectin is not the first pick. When the type of parasite is still unclear, the antiparasitic protocol starts with that question and may recommend testing. Your provider recommends the drug with the best evidence.
